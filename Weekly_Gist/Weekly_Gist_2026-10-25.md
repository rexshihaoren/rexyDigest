    *   **TL;DR:** RIDAR introduces a taxonomy and frameworks for detecting and correcting 14 types of research integrity violations in autonomous research agent trajectories, achieving 85.2% F1 on a benchmark and reducing remaining violations by 36.8% at runtime.
    *   **Takeaways:** Defines RIVT, a taxonomy of 14 research integrity violation types across the research lifecycle. RIDAR-post-hoc detects violations in agent trajectories with 85.2% F1 on RIV-Gold and finds 3,964 violations in 99.7% of 300 wild trajectories. RIDAR-runtime dynamically intervenes, reducing remaining violations at task completion by 36.8%. Code and data will be released to support further research on agent integrity.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioners deploying autonomous research agents, RIDAR provides concrete tools to monitor and correct integrity violations, turning abstract reliability concerns into measurable, actionable safeguards.

*   **Ehsan Saleh, Roozbeh Nahavandi, Sama Salarian, Raef Bassily, Andrew Perrault** — AfterDeleteBench: Auditing Post-Deletion Information Persistence in LLM Agents (Paper) — 2026-10-22 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31366)
    *   **TL;DR:** AfterDeleteBench shows that deleting original memory records from LLM agents often fails to remove information carried by derived state, with high recovery rates unless provenance-aware closure is applied.
    *   **Takeaways:** Post-deletion recovery is common: a single query recovers the deleted secret in about two thirds of cases for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, while never-exposed controls have zero recovery. Exact reconstruction from separately stored components under a public composition rule succeeds in 62-99% of cases across Qwen, Mistral, and Phi-3, even without a verbatim copy. Response interface and parser mismatch can cause target-bearing replies to be scored as failures; measurement must account for format. Provenance-aware closure eliminates observed recovery, but exact-literal write filters only block reinsertion, not already-present information. Deletion should be evaluated behaviorally at the agent and memory-system level, not just as a storage operation.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioners building long-term memory for AI agents must treat deletion as a system-level behavioral property, not a simple storage delete, or risk leaking supposedly removed information.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.7) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Qijin Zhang, Hanzhen Wang, Yuehan Zhao, Haoyu Dai, Yao Shu, Zhongxiang Dai, Shuang Qiu** — EpiSEM-Bench: Evaluating Safety and Evidence Recovery in LLM Agents under Missing Evidence (Paper) — 2026-10-22 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31650)
    *   **TL;DR:** EpiSEM-Bench is an executable benchmark with 300 matched task pairs that evaluates whether LLM agents safely handle missing safety-relevant evidence in tool responses, and whether they actively recover that evidence before acting.
    *   **Takeaways:** The benchmark reveals substantial variation among ten LLM agents in omission robustness, evidence acquisition, and appropriate action. Resistance to missing evidence does not necessarily translate into useful recovery; agents differ in whether they obtain and use missing facts. Recovery access reduces unsafe execution for all tested models, but active evidence-seeking behavior varies. An independent audit tool tests whether agents withhold prohibited actions while completing permissible tasks.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For a practitioner-philosopher, the paper shows that agent safety is not just about following returned facts but about the agent's ability to query missing reality-state variables, reinforcing the need for epistemic hygiene in AI as reality-code manipulators.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.6) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Aman Behera, Payal Payal** — Post-Audit Mirage: Identifiability Limits of Offline Agent Verification (Paper) — 2026-10-22 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31130)
    *   **TL;DR:** Offline verification of agent updates cannot distinguish safe from harmful deployment outcomes when verifier-visible evidence is matched, requiring deployment-time monitoring to resolve the identifiability gap.
    *   **Takeaways:** Offline tests alone can be structurally blind to post-deployment safety differences because safe and harmful lifecycles can produce identical verifier-visible evidence. The paper formalizes this as an observational-equivalence theorem with an error-abstention tradeoff. IdentifiedRangeMonitor uses fresh group-labelled harm outcomes to output deploy, hold, cannot determine, or unsupported. Monitoring recovers the missing distinction only in favorable settings; rare groups, small effects, or missing observations may leave decisions undecided. Practitioners should evaluate both the verifier's calculation validity and whether the available evidence actually supports deployment.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** A practitioner-philosopher should care because this paper demonstrates that the boundary of what can be known from offline audits is itself a formal limit, forcing a shift toward runtime monitoring and epistemic humility in agent governance.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.5) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Hossein Resani, Javen Qinfeng Shi** — Inside-JEPA: From Prediction to Relational Grounding in Latent World Models (Paper) — 2026-10-22 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31194)
    *   **TL;DR:** Inside-JEPA shows that adding relational channels to JEPA latent states and training them with an inverse-dynamics loss can ground entity relations without labels, improving planning for relational goals.
    *   **Takeaways:** JEPA latent prediction alone does not ground relational structure; inverse-dynamics supervision on relational channels significantly improves relation decoding. The approach works across Something-Something V2, RH20T robot episodes, and off-the-shelf detection/tracking, without relation labels. Action-conditioned predicted relational changes approach observed future relations, enabling one-step relational goal planning above prediction-only and frequency baselines. Shuffled labels, label-free objectives, and sparse action labels fail to ground channels, highlighting the role of inverse dynamics. The method uses 16 relational channels, making JEPA states more interpretable and actionable for model-based agents.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** This matters because it turns opaque latent world models into grounded relational simulators, letting an agent reason about entity relations for planning—a step toward more interpretable and steerable autonomous systems.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.0) | Topics: Agent**
