    *   **TL;DR:** An open midtraining recipe primes base models on long-context software-engineering data and a small share of agentic traces, improving agentic coding benchmarks and post-training robustness across model sizes.
    *   **Takeaways:** Midtraining at 256K context on packed repos, PRs, commits, and build logs plus agentic traces boosts agentic post-training performance. 500B midtraining tokens on Nemotron 3.5 Lightning raise SWE-bench Verified from 60.7 to 64.5 and Multilingual from 55.8 to 62.8. 280B midtraining tokens on Nemotron 3.5 Super improve DeepSWE from 11.7 to 15.7 and SWE-bench Verified from 72.9 to 74.3. Short fixed SFT runs give better proxy signals than base-model metrics, which do not correlate reliably with post-training results. The recipe is open and includes ablations, enabling replication for agentic model builders.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioner-philosophers treating AI agents as reality-code manipulators should adopt this open midtraining recipe to strengthen the tool-using substrate that steers simulated worlds or markets.

*   **Qingni Wang, Changdae Oh, Yuzhe Yang, Sharon Li, Jiachen Yang, Ang Li, Xin Eric Wang** — LEASH: Uncertainty-Aware Weak-to-Strong Escalation and Oversight for Computer-Use Agents (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22280)
    *   **TL;DR:** LEASH is a training-free framework that uses a weak model's latent uncertainty to decide when to escalate to a strong black-box computer-use agent and keeps it on a leash via a shadow observer, improving task success while reducing inference cost.
    *   **Takeaways:** LEASH escalates from weak to strong agents using uncertainty signals derived by comparing hidden states to past successful and failed trajectories. It reuses weak model activations already produced during action generation, requiring no extra forward pass or dedicated monitor training. After handoff, a shadow observer continues oversight of the strong black-box agent without needing internal access. On OSWorld, LEASH outperforms random escalation by 6.55–11.94 points and matches Claude Opus 5 success with 19.2% lower cost using Qwen3.5-35B-A3B.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** It shows how latent uncertainty can be turned into a control primitive for autonomous computer-use agents, letting practitioners steer expensive black-box models more cheaply and safely without surrendering oversight.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.8) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, Yang Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu, Fuli Feng, Chun Kai Ling, Tat-Seng Chua** — Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316)
    *   **TL;DR:** A reinforcement-learning-trained steering agent monitors LLM-based single- and multi-agent execution, detects goal drift, and intervenes via low-cost fast or high-cost slow paths to keep systems on track, improving performance and efficiency and generalizing to unseen tasks and executors.
    *   **Takeaways:** Introduces a steering agent that supervises tool-using LLM agents and multi-agent systems at runtime to correct goal drift. Uses a two-path intervention design: low-cost fast path for quick correction and high-cost slow path for deeper intervention. Jointly trains steering intelligence with reinforcement learning, combining pairwise ranking and GRPO, enabling learned drift assessment and intervention generation. Improves both performance and efficiency on information-seeking search tasks for single-agent and multi-agent setups. Learned steering generalizes out-of-domain, transfers to unseen executors, and supports controllable intervention frequency without retraining.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioner-philosophers should care because it operationalizes 'steering' as a learned control layer over LLM agents, a concrete step toward treating multi-agent execution as a governable computational process rather than an opaque emergent system.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.7) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Dongjie Cheng, Ruifeng Yuan, Ran Wei, Ning Song, Yongqi Li, Wenjie Li** — CoWorld Reasoner: Agentic Multimodal Reasoning with the Evolving Video World Model-Driven Harness (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21910)
    *   **TL;DR:** CoWorld Reasoner is an agentic framework that uses a Video World Model as a unified reasoning engine, with tools and skills enabling flexible visual imagination and co-evolution for improved multimodal spatiotemporal reasoning.
    *   **Takeaways:** Replaces fixed VWM pipelines with an agentic harness where a VLM decides when and how to generate visual evidence via tools. Introduces VLM-VWM Harness Co-Evolution that revises tools, skills, and code based on execution feedback without updating model weights. Outperforms direct VLM reasoning across seven benchmarks, with relative gains up to 13% on ERQA. Shows that flexible visual imagination through world models is a practical path to stronger multimodal agents.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioner-philosophers, it demonstrates how coupling an autonomous agent with a learned world model—effectively a mini-simulation of visual reality—can extend reasoning beyond direct perception, blurring the line between model and simulated environment.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.5) | Topics: Agent, Simulation**

*   **Maxence Faldor, Satinder Singh, Roberta Raileanu** — A team of Gemini 4 Argon agents beats Minecraft (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319)
    *   **TL;DR:** Four Gemini 4 Argon agents autonomously coordinated through chat and shared memory to write 2,783 programs and defeat Minecraft's Ender Dragon in 27 hours, demonstrating long-horizon multi-agent coding.
    *   **Takeaways:** First end-to-end autonomous completion of Minecraft by AI agents using only standard Mineflayer, no pre-scouted seeds or human tools. Agents wrote and debugged 2,783 programs over 27h14m, dying 605 times, sustaining long-horizon coding. Coordination via in-game chat and shared memory enabled role division, lesson sharing, and item recovery. Complex real-world software/enterprise tasks demand the same long-horizon coding and multi-agent coordination.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** This milestone demonstrates that multi-agent LLMs can sustain coherent, self-directed code manipulation inside a simulated world over day-long horizons, making them a concrete case study for 'reality-code manipulators' evolving within computable environments.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.4) | Topics: Agent, Simulation**

*   **Chenyuan Zhou, Yining Hong, Huang Huang, Yunfan Jiang, Wenlong Huang, David Held, Vitor Campagnolo Guizilini, Yue Wang, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu, Leonidas Guibas, Yejin Choi** — T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364)
    *   **TL;DR:** T2-z introduces test-time optimization of environment latents for robotic world models, enabling robots to infer hidden physical factors from few interactions, improving prediction and action success without supervision.
    *   **Takeaways:** Environment latents can capture unobservable physical properties (friction, mass) and rules, learned without explicit supervision. Alternating optimization between world model and environment latents lets latent structure emerge purely from world prediction. At test time, frozen world model adapts to unseen environments by optimizing a new latent from one or more interactions. Across sim and real tasks, reduces object prediction error from 15.20 to 7.00 and raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96%. Emergent latents represent individual and compounded factors across visual scenes.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** A practitioner-philosopher should care because this shows unlabeled latent factors of physical reality can be inferred and used for adaptation, supporting the view that environment dynamics are compressible into learnable latent codes—an ingredient for agents that treat the world as a queryable simulation.
    *   **CompositeScore (3.5) | Topics: Agent, Simulation**

*   **Hongchang Liu, Jian Liu, Yujuan Ding, Liang Peng, Xun Jiang, Fei Ma, Xing Xu, Yi Bin** — GeoState: Learning View-Decoupled Geometry States for 3D World Modeling (Paper) — 2026-10-15 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22343)
    *   **TL;DR:** GeoState introduces a view-decoupled geometry representation that separates scene dynamics from camera motion, enabling more stable and generalizable predictive 3D world models for autonomous agents.
    *   **Takeaways:** View-dependent geometry features entangle scene dynamics with camera motion; GeoState decouples them into a camera-independent state. A scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view geometry features, and a geometry decoder renders the state under arbitrary cameras. The autoregressive world model predicts future states without per-frame scale alignment, improving long-term consistency. Experiments on Waymo, KITTI, and nuScenes show improved performance and zero-shot cross-dataset generalization.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Predictive world models that decouple observer viewpoint from scene state move toward treating reality as a queryable, simulation-like representation—useful for embodied agents that must reason independently of their camera.
    *   **CompositeScore (3.2) | Topics: Agent, Simulation**
