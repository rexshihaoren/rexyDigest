*   **Yuyang Wu, Yang Liu, Zequn Xie, Junjie Wang, Dan Yang, Zehao Tan, Ding-Chu Zhang, Junming Yang, Yue Shen, Jian Wang, Jinjie Gu** — From Delegation to Coordination: Learning Efficient Multi-Agent Deep Research (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17041)
    *   **TL;DR:** LEAD-RL introduces contribution-aware credit assignment for joint training of main and subagents in multi-agent deep research, improving accuracy and reducing critical-path cost.
    *   **Takeaways:** LEAD-RL jointly trains main and subagents, unlike prior work that either optimizes only the main agent or shares the same team advantage with all agents. A training-time rubric scores each subagent report's research utility and gates its inherited team advantage, preventing misleading reports from being rewarded. The only team reward is final-answer correctness; no token, step, or speedup reward is used. Across five benchmarks and two model families, LEAD-RL improves accuracy and reduces critical-path cost; on BrowseComp it raises accuracy by 2.49 points and cuts cost by 24.5%.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioner-philosophers should care because contribution-aware credit assignment is a concrete mechanism for steering emergent multi-agent coordination without brittle reward shaping, aligning with the view that agent collectives can be tuned like computational systems with observable utility gradients.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.7) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Yang Zhou, Krish Agarwal, Zhuoming Chen, Zerui Wang, Zhenyu Zhang, Wenshuang Li, Xinpeng Wei, Zhichen Zeng, Jingyu Liu, Jue Wang, Sen Wu, Ziheng Jiang, Zhen Dong, Han Cai, Binhang Yuan, Ying Sheng, Ce Zhang, Bo Li, Beidi Chen** — Singularity-Bench: Can Agents Build Agents? (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16591)
    *   **TL;DR:** Singularity-Bench introduces an open-ended benchmark with six end-to-end tasks for autonomous AI development, showing that one frontier agent (Opus 5.5 with Claude Code) matches or exceeds human baselines with a Singularity-Index of 1.25, while other agents average 0.58.
    *   **Takeaways:** Singularity-Bench covers pre-training, mid-training, post-training, inference, agent harness, and cluster orchestration, requiring agents to implement core systems from minimal starting points. Red-teaming against OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications exposed reward-hacking in the Harbor framework, leading to Breakwater, which blocks all tested attacks. Across 11 agents using 7 frontier models and 2 harnesses, Opus 5.5 with Claude Code was the only one to match or beat human baselines on all six tasks. The benchmark aims to track when agents transition from assisting AI development to taking end-to-end responsibility for building stronger successors.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** A practitioner-philosopher should care because this quantifies the self-improvement capacity of AI agents—a threshold moment for recursive intelligence and the shaping of reality by autonomous code.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.7) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Tiansheng Huang, Ananth Balashankar, Virat Shejwalkar, Santosh Pande, Ling Liu** — Bait: Mitigating Harmful Agentic Reinforcement Learning via Probing Reward Function (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16369)
    *   **TL;DR:** The paper demonstrates that harmful agentic reinforcement learning can cheaply subvert LLM safety alignment and proposes Bait, a reward-probing defense that blocks harmful reward learning while preserving benign task learning.
    *   **Takeaways:** Agentic RL-as-a-service can be attacked for as little as $20 to completely subvert a model's safety alignment. Bait proactively probes the reward function to detect and mitigate harmful reward learning. The defense effectively blocks harmful rewards while still allowing learning from benign agentic tasks. Even adaptive attackers who know about Bait cannot perfectly bypass it due to a systematic challenge.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioner-philosophers should care because steering autonomous agents requires securing reward functions against adversarial manipulation, a core challenge when incentives are programmable.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.6) | Topics: Agent**

*   **swyx .ai** — [AINews] D-Day (Decision Models Day) (Blog) — 2026-11-12 — [https://latent.space/p/ainews-d-day-decision-models-day/](https://latent.space/p/ainews-d-day-decision-models-day/)
    *   **TL;DR:** Google's Gemini Decision 1 ushers in a new class of fast, cheap, calibrated single-pass decision models, with Cloudflare's hybrid architecture and fresh evals showing how to slash LLM calls and tokens in agentic systems.
    *   **Takeaways:** Gemini Decision 1 accepts up to 1,000 closed-set questions across modalities with 256K context, ~200ms end-to-end latency, confidence scores, and $0.10/M input tokens, beating Jev 1.14 on 5 of 7 shared benchmarks. Cloudflare's hybrid agent demo uses fast decision models for tool selection/log extraction and a large reasoning model only for diagnosis, reducing six LLM calls to one and ~40K tokens to 1.8K. Independent evals show Decision 1 ties Elephant 9B and Jev on Decision Index but lags Drex Max on Paperclip; cost-per-task and latency are now first-class metrics alongside accuracy. Fable 5.2 leads Agent Arena with +30.4% net improvement, while Gemini 4 Carbon draws latency complaints (22m low-effort vs 9m Opus high), and decision-model/agent evaluation is fragmenting by task type. Runway's Praxis-1 Apache 2.0 world action model ranks #1 on RoboLab-120, completing tasks 15.6x faster than GPT-6 Astra at 51x lower cost, showing decision/action models extend to embodied agents.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For a practitioner-philosopher treating AI + markets as reality-code manipulators, this shift to cheap, calibrated decision models offers a practical mechanism for reducing token/compute overhead in agent steering and hints at a future where 'reality code' is executed by specialized decision primitives.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.6) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Nguyen Minh Thang** — Contain, Conform, Compare (Extended Version): A Red-Teamed Evaluation Kit for Small Coding Agents and the Full Record of Its Experiments (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16485)
    *   **TL;DR:** The paper presents a red-teamed local evaluation kit for small coding agents, with safe container isolation and scorer-matching checks, plus a full public record of 9,660 agent runs and pre-registered experiments showing no confirmed prompt improvements.
    *   **Takeaways:** drydock-eval safely evaluates small coding agents in fresh containers at ~250 runs/hour on one RTX 5090, matching hosted scorer prompts. The kit passed 200 scripted escape tests and includes eight conformance checks to align local stack requests with the scorer configuration. 9,660 agent runs, 2,494 grading controls, and 72 serving measurements are released; prompts and agent bundles are withheld. A pre-registered five-arm experiment found no solve-rate change from four prompt divergences on E4B; three agent changes showed screens but failed confirmation. 65% of tasks were solved in none of eight runs, revealing high variance and sensitivity in small coding agent evaluation.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioners building or steering coding agents should adopt rigorous, safe, local evaluation with red-teaming and pre-registered experiments to avoid being misled by noisy single-number scores.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.6) | Topics: Agent**

*   **NovaSilicon Team** — AnalogLayoutWorld: An Open Benchmark for Agentic Analog Layout Design (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16991)
    *   **TL;DR:** AnalogLayoutWorld is an open benchmark with 40 expert-reviewed analog layout tasks where only 23.0% of agent attempts meet acceptance criteria, highlighting gaps in long-horizon physical design and the value of verification feedback and test-time scaling.
    *   **Takeaways:** 91.1% of attempts produce GDS layouts, but only 23.0% satisfy acceptance criteria—generation is not enough. Novara, an internally developed agent system, achieves the highest aggregate score. Test-time scaling under fixed budgets reveals reasoning efficiency and effective use of verification feedback as key differentiators. Open benchmark provides 40 expert-reviewed tasks with independent physical and post-layout electrical verification and expert co-designed quality metrics.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** Practitioner-philosophers should track agentic layout systems because they show how tool-using LLMs navigate physical constraint spaces—an early laboratory for steering reality-manipulating code under hard verification.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.5) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Doyoung Kim, Byeongguk Jeon, Kailin Zhang, Sherry Yang** — Experience-Handoff Reinforcement Learning for Recursive Self-Improvement (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16409)
    *   **TL;DR:** The paper proposes ACERL, which trains LLM agents to co-optimize action, reflection, and playbook curation by evaluating experience-handoff artifacts through their downstream effect on future rollouts, improving held-out agentic performance.
    *   **Takeaways:** ACERL uses scalar reward plus environment information to train models that hand off experience (e.g., playbooks) to future rollouts. The method evaluates candidate handoff artifacts by their downstream effect on future actor rollouts, enabling gradient updates for recursive self-improvement. Co-optimizing action generator, insight reflector, and playbook curator under a fixed scaffold outperforms baselines on agentic benchmarks. Results suggest agent training should optimize model-written experience that future rollouts inherit, not just immediate actions.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioners building self-improving agent systems, this paper shows how to make experience handoff a learned, gradient-optimized component rather than a manually engineered harness, pushing toward more autonomous recursive improvement.
    *   **CompositeScore (4.5) | Topics: Agent**

*   **Chenkai Hu, Yingqian Zhang, Ming Xie, Yi Fang, Chengxiang Jin, Chao Li** — HALight: Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Traffic Signal Control under Lane-Level Missing Observations (Paper) — 2026-11-12 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16574)
    *   **TL;DR:** HALight is a heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning framework for traffic signal control that handles lane-level missing observations via communication and sequential updates, outperforming baselines on real-world road networks.
    *   **Takeaways:** Supports heterogeneous intersections with intersection-specific observation and action modeling. Uses a lightweight one-hop neighborhood communication module to compensate for missing lane-level observations. Employs sequential policy updates to improve coordination among heterogeneous agents. Outperforms homogeneous and heterogeneous baselines on two real-world road networks.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For those steering decentralized agents in imperfect simulations of physical systems, HALight offers a concrete template for heterogeneity and missing data, though it does not engage the simulation hypothesis itself.
    *   **CompositeScore (3.0) | Topics: Agent**
