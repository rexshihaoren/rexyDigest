*   **Yimeng Liu, Mi Zhang, Younsuk Dong, Zhichao Cao** — Mimir: Physics-Grounded LLM Agents for Long-Horizon Irrigation Control (Paper) — 2026-10-01 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02038](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02038)
    *   **TL;DR:** Mimir demonstrates how to build long-horizon LLM agents for physical control by pairing semantic reasoning with a deterministic physics simulator, fast verified revision, and slow persistent contextual principles, achieving lower aggregate control cost and about 51% less irrigation than historical schedules.
    *   **Takeaways:** Long-horizon physical control differs from episodic LLM tasks because actions alter future states and errors compound, requiring explicit feedback loops. Mimir uses two repair timescales: a fast structured physical interface with deterministic simulation for numerical checking and revision, and a slow consolidation of recurrent failures into persistent principles. Keeping the physical model, evaluator, and execution constraints immutable preserves ground truth and safety while allowing LLM contextual adaptation. Ablations show forward simulation, verified revision, and persistent context each reduce control cost, while increasing LLM size does not monotonically improve performance.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioner-philosophers, Mimir operationalizes a boundary between semantic reasoning and computable physical truth, showing how to steer agents without letting them rewrite the reality-code that governs safe execution.
