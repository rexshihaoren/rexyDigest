*   **Ruizhe Li, Licheng Zhang, Benfeng Xu, Mingxuan Du, Zheren Fu, Weidong Chen** — When Your Agent Opens the Chat App: Agent-Controlled Search over Raw Chat Logs Rivals Structured Memory (Paper) — 2026-08-13 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12888)
    *   **TL;DR:** Agent-controlled lexical search over unmodified chat logs, without any semantic memory structure, rivals or beats graph- and tree-based memory systems on conversational memory benchmarks, suggesting structured memory may be overkill for precise QA.
    *   **Takeaways:** ReFind indexes raw chat logs lexically at turn granularity and builds no embeddings, summaries, or knowledge graphs. Agent-controlled keyword search with session-aware rank fusion, local context expansion, temporal narrowing, and skipping already-inspected sessions reaches 58.2 mean accuracy on MemoryAgentBench, above HippoRAG 2's 53.2. Controlled comparisons show the gain comes from agent control and chat-native controls, not just single-shot BM25. The same interface achieves 93.2±3.3 and 89.3±6.0 on LongMemEval-S/M with GPT-5-mini. For precise, evidence-grounded questions over chat archives, much of the benefit credited to elaborate memory structures is recoverable with controllable search over the raw record.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioner-philosophers treating agents as reality-code manipulators, this suggests the most effective memory may be direct, controllable access to the raw log—less structure, more agency—echoing a preference for inspectable, recomputable substrates over lossy world models.

*   **Latent Space** — [AINews] How to steal a Reasoning Trace (Blog) — 2026-08-12 — [https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-how-to-steal-a-reasoning-trace](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-how-to-steal-a-reasoning-trace)
    *   **TL;DR:** AINews/Latent Space covers a disclosed vulnerability that allows decoding encrypted frontier-model reasoning traces by replaying signed blocks to weaker models, exposing hidden chain-of-thought and leaked secrets in public traces.
    *   **Takeaways:** A cross-frontier API vulnerability allowed extracting signed hidden reasoning by replaying blocks to weaker models with prefills, with recovered token counts matching billed thinking tokens. Scanning ~7,000 public traces found 62 API keys, 33 email addresses, 33 passwords, and other secrets only in decoded reasoning blocks. Hidden CoT is not a reliable monitoring interface: decoded reasoning may be terse, multilingual, or 'neuralese'; tool surfaces can re-expose internal-format thinking. The episode splits between serious privacy/safety problem and impractical mass theft for training, but makes public trace sharing risky and demands stronger sandboxing/telemetry. Practical guidance: avoid sharing public traces with reasoning blobs; labs need stronger guarantees around sandboxing, telemetry, and tool surfaces.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** A practitioner-philosopher should care because hidden reasoning was shown to be decodable, correlating with billed thinking tokens and leaking secrets, so assumptions about private chain-of-thought for agent steering or monitoring are unstable; if reality is computable, even 'encrypted' internal computation leaks through side channels and model interfaces.

    *   **TL;DR:** This paper formally defines causal world models that integrate object-centric representations, entity interactions, and structural causal models to support agents' prediction, planning, and decision-making, and clarifies identifiability of their components from data.
    *   **Takeaways:** Causal world models should capture entity properties, entity-to-entity, and entity-to-environment interactions, not just generative predictions. The paper connects world modeling to causal representation learning, object-centric learning, causal discovery, and model-based decision-making. Identifiability analysis shows when components of a causal world model can be recovered from observations and up to what equivalence. This grounds world models in causal structure, enabling more reliable out-of-distribution generalization and informed decision-making for agents.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For those building or steering agents, this offers a formal blueprint to move from black-box predictors to interpretable causal simulations of the environment, directly enabling debugging and control.

*   **Guo An, Zijing Wu, Honghua Dong, Yuhao Yan, Zixuan Gui, Haochong Chen, Shanzhao Ruan, Xiang Wang, Yurong Ling, Qi Tian** — Diagnosing JEPA World Models with Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency (Paper) — 2026-08-13 — [https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12939](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12939)
    *   **TL;DR:** The paper introduces Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency (ACPC), a diagnostic that measures divergence between clean and visually perturbed rollouts in JEPA world models, bounding perturbation-induced changes in multi-step prediction error and planner cost.
    *   **Takeaways:** JEPA latent predictions do not guarantee robustness to visual perturbations. ACPC measures divergence between clean and perturbed histories rolled forward under the same action sequence. The divergence bounds perturbation-induced changes in multi-step prediction error and planner cost. Invariance Radius (IR) and Separation Rate (SR) provide complementary diagnostics, validated on LeWM and PLDM.
    *   **Implication for Rex Ren:** For practitioners building or steering world-model agents, ACPC offers a principled way to detect when latent representations collapse under visual distortions, preventing silent failures in action-conditioned planning.
